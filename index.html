About Me

I am a marine scientist interested in how aquatic ecosystems function, respond to environmental change, and inform conservation. My research is rooted in marine biogeochemistry, where I investigate how microorganisms acquire phosphorus and in doing so, influence nutrient cycling across marine and freshwater environments. Currently, I am an Alaska Sea Grant State Fellow, hosted by the NOAA Protected Resources Division in Juneau, Alaska. Through this work, I hope to advance science-informed conservation and the protection of marine ecosystems.

Research

Microbial Phosphorus Acquisition

This work examines how microorganisms acquire, transform, and cycle phosphorus across aquatic environments, combining microbial ecology, analytical chemistry, and molecular approaches in laboratory and field-based studies.

Marine Mammal Protection & Response

In this role, I support the large whale and pinniped entanglement response programs, the marine mammal stranding efforts, and recovery efforts for the W. distinct population segment Steller sea lions. I am developing the skills to conduct informal consultations under the Endangered Species Act Section 7.

Synthetic Phosphorus Compounds in Aquatic Environments

Examining the distribution, microbial transformation, and environmental fate of synthetic organophosphorus compounds.
